RunAHomeLab is a research-driven publication. Hardware buying guides are not presented as hands-on reviews unless a page explicitly documents a RunAHomeLab test and its method.

The goal is not to sell certainty we do not possess. It is to help readers make better decisions from traceable evidence, clear assumptions, and visible uncertainty.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Official specification** | A fact from the hardware manufacturer, software project, standards body, or other primary source. |
| **Independent testing** | A third party physically tested or benchmarked the relevant product or configuration. |
| **Community evidence** | An attributable owner or operator report. Useful for finding patterns, but not a controlled test. |
| **Analysis** | A RunAHomeLab conclusion derived from cited evidence. It is not presented as a measurement. |
| **Hypothesis** | A plausible explanation that the available evidence does not yet confirm. |
| **Price checked** | Price or availability checked on the date shown. Prices and seller configurations can change after publication. |

One community report is described as one report. It is not converted into “users report” or treated as proof that every unit is affected. A manufacturer's acoustic or performance claim is not presented as an independent measurement.

## How hardware is researched

For hardware guides, RunAHomeLab aims to:

1. verify processor and platform facts against official specifications;
2. verify the exact system configuration against the manufacturer and retailer listing;
3. review independent testing when it exists;
4. search owner and technical-community reports for recurring compatibility or reliability issues;
5. separate product-level capabilities from CPU-level capabilities;
6. state workload assumptions and important unknowns;
7. check that an affiliate destination still matches the model and configuration described.

If the evidence is insufficient, the recommendation remains conditional or is removed. An exact product should not be recommended merely because an affiliate link exists.

## Technical guides

Commands and procedures are checked against current upstream documentation where possible. Version-specific behavior, workarounds, and community observations are labelled accordingly. Snapshots, backups, redundancy, and high availability are treated as different concepts.

Technical guidance can become outdated. Check the page's last-reviewed date and the linked upstream documentation before applying a destructive or production-critical change.

## Affiliate independence

Some pages contain affiliate links. **As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.** Affiliate relationships do not change the evidence standard, and a recommendation should still make sense if its commission were zero.

Amazon prices, sellers, availability, and configurations can change. RunAHomeLab generally avoids publishing static Amazon prices and asks readers to verify the exact listing before buying.

## Corrections

If a factual error is found, the goal is to correct the public page, update its reviewed date when a substantive evidence review occurred, and preserve uncertainty rather than silently replacing one unsupported claim with another.

Corrections can be reported through [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/sasha-voskolovych/) or [GitHub](https://github.com/Vosikkk/site-for).
